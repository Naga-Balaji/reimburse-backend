Reimburse

Take-home for AI Planet · Nortex Industries brief
The problem

Every travelling employee loses 25–30 minutes per trip to a paper-form ritual

Chaitanya returns from a Bengaluru trip. His inbox has 15 emails: Uber receipts, a hotel folio, a duplicate resend, a colleague's expense forwarded to him, a promo, a payment failure notice, a flight ticket paid by the company card. He opens a blank Excel form and manually assembles a claim. Then chases Finance for 2 weeks.

  • Manual assembly from a messy inbox — the 25 minutes
  • Errors leak through — non-reimbursables get claimed, duplicates get paid
  • Silent workflow — nobody knows where the money is until it arrives
The policy the app must enforce

NTX-HR-POL-11 Rev 4 · effective 01 Apr 2026

Lodging — Tier‑1 ₹6k / Tier‑2 ₹4k / Tier‑3 ₹2.8k per night
Meals — Tier‑1 ₹1.5k / others ₹1k per day
Advance — up to 60% cap of estimated cost
Business meals — >₹2k needs HoD approval
Non‑reimbursable — laundry, mini‑bar, spa, gym, alcohol, personal phone, fines, insurance

Primary aim
Remove the 25 minutes. Enforce the policy automatically. Give every rupee a live audit trail — from advance disbursement to final settlement, visible to everyone who needs it.
Build for the messy inbox, not a cleaner form.
Must-have features

What the app does end-to-end

  • Import from Inbox — parses .eml files, filters noise, splits hotel folios
  • Deterministic parsers for Uber, MakeMyTrip, hotel invoices
  • LLM fallback via OpenRouter for unknown senders
  • Policy engine — lodging caps + non-reimbursables at save-time
  • Two-stage approval — TR pre-trip, Claim post-trip
  • Tiered approvals per policy §2 (Manager → HoD → Div → MD)
  • AI Verification Assistant — advisor for approvers, never auto-decides
  • Employee Context panel — past history, plan-vs-actual for reviewers
  • Advance disbursement audit as a distinct Finance sub-tree
  • Finance ↔ Employee thread when an advance is held
  • Proof document viewer — inline .png / .pdf / .eml rendering
  • Admin oversight — policy view, org tree, system stats

Design principle: the shape of the code mirrors product thinking. core/parsers.py is a first-class file (not buried in utils/) because reading the inbox is the product. core/llm.py is separate from views.py because AI is an integration, not the core.

Tech stack & why over the alternatives
Django + DRF — fastest way to expose a domain with role-based permissions. Not Node/Express: DRF's viewsets + queryset-level RBAC compress a week of Node scaffolding into a day.
React + Tailwind — component reuse, dense enterprise UI. Not Next.js: no SSR needed; this is an internal tool, not a public site.
OpenRouter (LLM) — multi-model with fallback. Not direct OpenAI/Anthropic SDK: OpenRouter lets me swap models without code changes.
PostgreSQL via DATABASE_URL — one-line dev-to-prod switch. Not MongoDB: this is relational — approvals, hierarchies, joins.
Token auth (DRF) — bearer tokens, no CSRF/session complexity. Not JWT: overkill; DRF's built-in Token is enough.
Inter font · Atlassian palette — financial-grade tabular numerals. Not Material/Bootstrap: they feel like consumer apps, not enterprise SaaS.
Railway (backend) — auto-detects Django + Postgres. Not AWS/Heroku: 30-min deploy from GitHub with zero YAML.
Vercel (frontend) — auto rebuilds on push. Not Netlify: identical DX, Vercel just felt smoother for CRA.
